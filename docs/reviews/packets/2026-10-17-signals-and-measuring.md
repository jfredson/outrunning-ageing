# The eight branches

Every branch is marked required, and a branch counts as handled in the year its second treatment reaches clinical use (`handled_when = 2`). The run's escape year is the latest handled year across all eight, plus the drawn lag. The treatments per branch are counted below; the ones in this packet are given in full further down.

- **Worn-out cells** (`worn-out-cells`): Cells that have stopped dividing but will not die, and sit in the tissue giving off signals that inflame their neighbours. They build up with age; clearing them makes old mice healthier and longer-lived. Treatments tracked: 5, of which 0 already in clinical use. What would move this branch: A controlled trial in people in which a drug that kills these cells improves an age-related condition on its primary outcome, or a regulator's approval of one.
- **Damaged DNA** (`damaged-dna`): Mutations that accumulate in a cell's DNA over a lifetime, and the shortening of the protective caps on the ends of chromosomes each time a cell divides. Treatments tracked: 4, of which 0 already in clinical use. What would move this branch: A gene therapy for a single ageing-related mutation with a measured result in people, or a treatment that lengthens chromosome ends in a living animal with no rise in cancer.
- **The cell's settings** (`cell-settings`): The chemical marks that tell a cell which of its genes to read, which drift with age so that cells slowly forget what they are. Resetting them part of the way has reversed signs of age in animals. Treatments tracked: 5, of which 0 already in clinical use. What would move this branch: A measured reversal of a marker of biological age in a person after a brief, controlled dose of reprogramming genes, in a peer-reviewed report.
- **Broken proteins and waste** (`broken-proteins`): Misfolded proteins, the failure of the cell's recycling of its own worn parts, and the waste and cross-links between proteins that build up inside and between cells. Treatments tracked: 5, of which 2 already in clinical use. What would move this branch: A drug that clears a kind of waste throughout the body, not one organ, with a healthy-years or function result in a controlled trial in people.
- **Energy and fuel sensing** (`energy-and-fuel`): The cell's power plants, which fail with age, and the pathways that sense how much food is coming in, which run wrong with age and are the target of the drugs most often said to slow ageing. Treatments tracked: 8, of which 1 already in clinical use. What would move this branch: A controlled trial in people in which a drug that acts on these pathways keeps the people taking it free of age-related disease longer than those on placebo, by the margin the trial was designed to detect.
- **Replacing lost cells and parts** (`replacing-parts`): The reserve of cells that replace lost tissue runs out with age, tissue goes unreplaced and organs fail. The route around that is to supply the parts: stem cells, organs from gene-edited animals, organs grown outside the body. Treatments tracked: 7, of which 1 already in clinical use. What would move this branch: A regulator's approval of an organ from a gene-edited animal or an organ grown from a person's own cells for use outside a trial, or a stem cell therapy restoring a lost function in a controlled trial.
- **Inflammation and the body's messaging** (`inflammation-and-signalling`): Low-grade inflammation that never switches off, the gut's community of microbes going out of balance, and the signals cells send each other through the blood going wrong with age. Treatments tracked: 5, of which 1 already in clinical use. What would move this branch: A measured effect in people of exchanging or diluting the blood's plasma, or an anti-inflammatory drug with a healthy-years result in a controlled trial.
- **Measuring ageing** (`measuring-age`): The tools that say whether any of the above worked without waiting decades: biological age clocks read from blood or saliva, blood and imaging markers, and whether a regulator will accept any of them as proof that a treatment slowed ageing. Treatments tracked: 5, of which 0 already in clinical use. What would move this branch: A regulator accepting a marker of ageing as a trial endpoint, or recognising ageing itself as a condition a drug can be approved to treat.

## Treatment 1 of 10 in this packet: A drug that acts only on inflammation is approved to prevent heart attacks
Record id: `an-anti-inflammatory-drug-is-approved-to-prevent-heart-attacks`. Branch: `inflammation-and-signalling`. Horizon the record gives: mid.

### What it is
Low-grade inflammation that never switches off is one of the twelve published kinds of ageing damage (the "hallmarks"), and
for decades the question was whether damping it, with no change to cholesterol or blood
pressure, would prevent the diseases of age. In 2017 a trial of 10,061 people who had already had
a heart attack showed that canakinumab, an antibody against one inflammatory signal, cut further
heart attacks and strokes by about a sixth and, unexpectedly, lung cancer deaths; the regulator
declined to approve it for the heart because the benefit was modest and infections rose. The
answer came from an old gout pill: low-dose colchicine cut heart attacks and strokes by about a
third in a 2020 trial of 5,522 people, and the United States regulator approved it for that
purpose on 16 June 2023, the first anti-inflammatory drug approved to prevent heart disease.

### What would count (the test)
A controlled trial in people reports that a drug acting on inflammation alone, with no effect on
cholesterol, blood pressure, blood sugar or weight, lowers heart attacks, strokes or deaths more
than placebo, in a peer-reviewed report. For the clinic rung: a regulator approves such a drug to
prevent an age-related disease. For wide use: prescribed for that purpose in several countries to
a substantial share of eligible people.

Who or what settles it: The peer-reviewed trial report; the regulator's approval record; published uptake figures for the wide-use rung

## Treatment 2 of 10 in this packet: Blocking one inflammatory messenger protein makes old animals live longer
Record id: `blocking-one-messenger-protein-makes-old-animals-live-longer`. Branch: `inflammation-and-signalling`. Horizon the record gives: mid.

### What it is
Cells talk to each other through proteins in the blood, and some of those messages go wrong with
age. Interleukin-11 is one such messenger, rising with age and driving scarring and
inflammation. In 2024 a Singapore and London group reported that mice bred without it, or old
mice given an antibody that blocks it from 75 weeks of age (about 55 in human years), lived about
a quarter longer, with less frailty and fewer tumours. It is among the largest lifespan gains
from a single drug started late in life in mice. Antibodies against the same protein were in
early trials in people for lung scarring, not for ageing, when this was written.

### What would count (the test)
An old living animal given a drug that blocks a single messenger protein in the blood, started
in the second half of its life, afterwards lives longer than untreated controls, in a
peer-reviewed report. The first-human rung needs a controlled trial in which people given such a
drug improve on an age-related measure (frailty, muscle, a validated clock) more than placebo. For
the clinic rung: a regulator approves such a drug for an age-related condition.

Who or what settles it: Peer-reviewed reports; the trial registry; the regulator's approval record for the clinic rung

## Treatment 3 of 10 in this packet: Replacing the liquid part of a person's blood slows an age-related disease
Record id: `replacing-a-persons-plasma-slows-an-age-related-disease`. Branch: `inflammation-and-signalling`. Horizon the record gives: mid.

### What it is
If old blood carries signals that hold the body back, then taking out the liquid part (the
plasma) and replacing it with salt water and a blood protein should help, and in 2020 a Berkeley
group showed that it did in old mice: one such exchange improved their muscle repair, liver and
brain. The procedure, plasma exchange, has been used in hospitals for decades for other reasons.
In people the record so far is a 2020 Spanish and American trial of 347 people with Alzheimer's
disease (322 of them treated and followed), in which those given monthly plasma exchange for over a year declined more slowly on
tests of daily function than those given a sham procedure. It is a first human on an age-related
disease, with the caution that the trial had several measures and not all moved.

### What would count (the test)
A controlled trial in people reports that replacing or diluting the plasma slows the course of
an age-related disease, or improves an age-related function, more than a sham procedure on a
pre-stated main measure, in a peer-reviewed report. The animal rung is the same in a living
animal. For the clinic rung: a regulator approves plasma exchange or dilution for an age-related
condition, or a named clinical setting gives it for that purpose in routine care and publishes
that practice.

Who or what settles it: The peer-reviewed trial report; the regulator's approval record, or a named clinical setting's published practice, for the clinic rung

## Treatment 4 of 10 in this packet: Young blood rejuvenates an old animal
Record id: `young-blood-rejuvenates-an-old-animal`. Branch: `inflammation-and-signalling`. Horizon the record gives: far.

### What it is
In 2005 a Stanford group joined the circulations of a young and an old mouse, an old surgical
trick called parabiosis, and found that the old mouse's muscle and liver stem cells woke up and
repaired damage like a young animal's. The finding started a search for the factors in young
blood that do it and the factors in old blood that stop it, and a cottage industry of selling
young plasma to old people. Infusions of young plasma into people with Alzheimer's disease were
reported safe in 2019 in a tiny trial built to test safety, not benefit (thinking did not change;
caregivers rated daily function better, an unconfirmed result), and no controlled trial has found
a benefit on an age-related function. A 2025 Stanford trial in 38 older people having a joint
replacement found that four infusions of a young-donor plasma fraction changed their immune and
inflammatory response to the surgery, which is not a benefit to an age-related function. The leaf is at the animal rung and the test asks for a person to measurably improve,
not to receive the infusion.

### What would count (the test)
An old living animal given blood or plasma from a young one afterwards has a measurably
rejuvenated tissue function compared with untreated old controls, in a peer-reviewed report. The
first-human rung needs a controlled trial in which older people given young plasma, or a defined
factor from it, improve on an age-related measure more than placebo. For the clinic rung: a
regulator approves such a treatment for an age-related condition.

Who or what settles it: Peer-reviewed reports; the regulator's approval record for the clinic rung

### The stage now: shown in animals
Reached 2005-02-17. Record that settles it: https://doi.org/10.1038/nature03260

exchanging or diluting plasma, and that is the companion leaf.

## Treatment 7 of 10 in this packet: A regulator accepts a marker of ageing as proof that a treatment slowed it
Record id: `a-regulator-accepts-a-marker-of-ageing-as-proof-a-treatment-worked`. Branch: `measuring-age`. Horizon the record gives: mid.

### What it is
Every treatment on this tree faces the same wall: to be approved for ageing, it must be shown to
slow ageing, and the only accepted way to show that is to wait for people to get sick or die. A
marker that a regulator accepted instead, a clock or a panel of blood proteins that a treatment
had to move, would cut a decade off every trial. Regulators do this in other fields (blood
pressure stands in for strokes, viral load for AIDS), through formal programmes that qualify a
marker on evidence. No marker of ageing has been qualified by any regulator, and as of 2026 none
had been submitted far enough through the United States programme to be listed. Several
foundations and companies say they are working toward it.

### What would count (the test)
A regulator's formal programme qualifies a marker of ageing (a clock, a protein panel, a
functional measure) as a stand-in for the outcome a trial would otherwise wait for (what the
regulators call a surrogate endpoint), so that a trial can be designed to move it and an
approval can rest on that, in the regulator's own published record; that is the clinic rung for
this leaf. The first-human rung is reached when a regulator agrees in writing, in a registered
trial's record, to a marker of ageing as the trial's main measure, before the trial runs.
There is no animal rung for a regulatory act, and the idea-only rung means neither has happened.

Who or what settles it: The regulator's own qualification record or its written agreement to the trial's main measure; the trial registry entry that names the agreed endpoint

### What it is
A single number for a person's biological age hides that their heart may be old and their liver
young. In 2023 a Stanford group measured thousands of proteins in the blood of over five
thousand people, assigned each protein to the organ that makes it, and read an age for eleven
organs per person. About one person in five had at least one organ ageing fast, and a fast-ageing
heart predicted heart failure, a fast-ageing brain predicted dementia, over the following fifteen
years. It is the kind of reading a trial of a repair treatment would want: not whether the
person is younger, but whether the organ the treatment aimed at is. It is a research tool, not a
test anyone can order from a doctor.

### What would count (the test)
A panel of blood proteins read as an age for each of several organs predicts disease of that
organ better than calendar age, in two or more independent groups of at least a thousand people
followed for years, in peer-reviewed reports. For the clinic rung: a regulator clears such a panel
as a medical test, or a named health system uses it in routine care and publishes that practice.

Who or what settles it: Peer-reviewed validation reports; the regulator's clearance record, or a named health system's published practice, for the clinic rung

### The record's notes
The protein approach and the DNA-mark clocks (the companion leaf) are the two main families of
ageing marker; this one is newer and has the organ-by-organ reading the other lacks. Neither is
a medical test yet. The regulator-acceptance leaf is where either would have to go to count for
a trial.
Checked 2026-10-17 against the papers' abstracts: the 2023 report (Oh, Wyss-Coray and colleagues, Nature, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06802-1)
estimated organ ages in five independent cohorts with 5,676 adults in all. The 2025 follow-up (Oh, Wyss-Coray and colleagues, Nature
Medicine, online 2025-07-09, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-03798-1) is from the same group: organ ages from 44,498 people in
the UK Biobank were linked to disease starting within 17 years. An independent group (Goeminne, Gladyshev and colleagues, Cell Metabolism, online 2024-11-01,
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2024.10.005) built organ-specific protein ages from over 50,000 people in the UK Biobank and other
cohorts and reported that they predict disease in those organs. On the test as worded (two or more independent groups of at least a
thousand people), the second independent group would date from 2024-11-01. This record has not ruled whether the test is met, or
from when, and the stage and its date (2023-12-06) are unchanged. The abstracts do not state a comparison with calendar age, which
the test also asks for; the full texts were not read for it.

# From the method document: what the computation does and the parameters

## What the computation does

1. **Every tracked leaf supplies a year it reaches the clinic** (`in-clinical-use`). A leaf already there, or beyond, supplies the year it happened. A leaf below draws a year from its estimate: a triangle over the earliest, likely and latest years, so the likely year is the most probable draw and the ends are possible but rare (`docs/record-schema.md`, "How a leaf's state feeds the headline").
2. **Every branch counts as handled in the year its `handled_when`-th leaf reaches the clinic.** With the default of two, a branch is handled when its second treatment is in clinical use. The branch's leaf years are sorted and the second is taken.
3. **The run's escape year is the latest handled year across the branches marked `required`, plus a drawn lag** for the treatments to spread from the clinic to ordinary people, so that the year is when a person who keeps up with the best available care actually gets the gain, not when the paper came out.
4. **Many thousands of runs**, each with fresh draws, give a spread of escape years. The headline reads three numbers off it: the median year; the share of runs with an escape year before 2050; the share of runs with an escape year after 2100, said in public as "not this century". The 5th and 95th percentiles are reported as the spread ("anywhere from 2044 to not this century" when the 95th is past 2100).

Two honesty rules from the kickoff, repeated on the public method page: the combination rule (all required branches handled, plus a lag) is the project's judgment, and the strongest alternative, a weighted sum in which some branches matter more, is recorded below with why it was not chosen; and the ranges are the project's reading of the evidence, revised only with a dated reason and a source, never because a number looked wrong.

## A worked run, by hand

For the method page, once the leaves exist, one simplified run is written out so a reader can follow it: eight branches, two leaves each, one drawn year per leaf, the second-smallest year per branch, the largest of the eight, plus the lag. Until then this is the shape of it:

| branch | leaf years drawn this run | handled (second leaf) |
|---|---|---|
| Worn-out cells | 2036, 2041 | 2041 |
| ... | ... | ... |
| Measuring ageing | 2033, 2047 | 2047 |
| latest handled year across the required branches | | 2047 |
| plus a drawn lag of 9 years | | **2056**, this run's escape year |

## The parameters (proposed 2026-10-16, ruled by John the same day; parameter 2 flagged for the outside review)

| # | Choice | Confidence | Standard or judgment | Strongest alternative and its cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The draw for a leaf below the clinic is a triangle** over the rung's `earliest`, `likely` and `latest` years. | Moderate. Three numbers are what a person can argue about in public ("no earlier than, most likely, no later than"), and the triangle is the simplest shape that uses all three and nothing else. | Judgment; a common choice where only three-point estimates exist. | A bell curve centred on `likely` with the ends two standard deviations out: smoother, but it never says "no earlier than", and the earliest year on a treatment with no human result is a real floor. Or a probability per year, as The Belt Equation carries: more expressive, harder to argue about. |
| 2 | **The lag from the clinic to ordinary people is a triangle over 3, 8 and 15 years**, drawn once per run and shared by every branch. | Low on the numbers; moderate on the shape. New drugs take years to reach wide use after approval (price, guidelines, supply); the figures are a first guess with no source and are flagged for the outside review. | Judgment. | A lag per branch, drawn separately: more honest about the difference between a pill and a transplant, at the cost of eight more guesses. Or no lag, with the clinic rung as the arrival: simpler, and it would count the year a drug is approved as the year everyone has it. |
| 3 | **All required branches must be handled** (the latest handled year is taken). | Moderate; the kickoff's rule. It says in the model what the hallmarks papers say in prose: ageing is many kinds of damage, and outrunning it means dealing with each. | Judgment. | A weighted sum, in which the branches with the most deaths behind them count for more and a branch could be skipped: it would pull the year in and would need eight weights nobody has published. Recorded here, as the kickoff asks, with why it was not chosen: a weight is a second judgment on top of the ranges, and the ranges are already the arguable part. |
| 4 | **The fixed dates are 2050 and 2100.** The chance before 2050 is the second number; the share after 2100 is "not this century". | High on 2050: the kickoff's reason stands (the loudest promises land before it, and most readers under fifty expect to see it). High on 2100 as the century's end. Both are project constants, changed only at a yearly review and logged. | Standard: the kickoff's. | A reader's own life expectancy (The Belt Equation's framing), deferred to version two as the kickoff says. |
| 5 | **200,000 runs.** | High. The percentages then wobble by about a tenth of a point from seed to seed, far below any rounding the site shows, and the run takes under a second. | Standard: "many thousands" with margin; the same as The Quiet Galaxy. | One million runs for a twentieth of a point, or 20,000 for noise that would show in a diff of the JSON. |
| 6 | **A fixed seed, 2004** (the year the phrase "longevity escape velocity" became known). | High that the seed is fixed: the published numbers then reproduce exactly on any machine, and `data/headline.json` changes only when a record changes, so every change to the headline is a visible diff with a changelog line. Low on the particular value, which can be any number. | Standard practice for a published computation. | No seed: the numbers wobble on every run and every build produces a diff. |
| 7 | **Independent draws**: every leaf's year and the lag are drawn without any link between them. | Moderate. Nothing published gives a rule for how a breakthrough in one branch speeds another, and a rule invented here would be a third judgment. The outside review of the ranges is the place to raise it. | Judgment. | Linked draws (a shared "the field is going well" factor): more realistic, since the branches share money, regulators and tools, and it would widen the spread; it needs a parameter with no source. |
| 8 | **The readiness rule: `run` refuses until every branch has at least `handled_when` tracked leaves**, naming the branches short of it. | High. Without that many leaves a branch can never be handled and every run would say "never", which would be the shape of the record and not of the world. | Judgment, following the siblings' "stub refuses until the factors exist". | Treat a short branch as handled at its last leaf's year, or skip it: either hides the gap. |
| 9 | **The result is written to `data/headline.json` and committed**, and `scripts/export.py` reads it. | Moderate. It is data the site reads, it belongs with the data it is computed from, and committing it means a headline change is a reviewable diff. The same choice The Quiet Galaxy made and John ruled there. | Judgment. | Compute at build time inside the export script with no file: nothing to commit, but the headline could change without a changelog line. |
| 10 | **Years past 2300 are refused in the record; a drawn year past 2100 counts as "not this century"** and is otherwise left as it is in the spread. | Moderate. "Never" has to be expressible, and a far year is how a three-point estimate says it; 2300 is far enough that nothing is lost. | Judgment. | A separate "chance it never happens" field per leaf: cleaner in meaning, one more number to argue about, and it needs a rule for combining with the years. |
