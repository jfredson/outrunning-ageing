1. **2026-11-06. The United States drug regulator is investigating cancers in children given the gene
   therapy Zolgensma.** Its notice says three reported tumours involved one cancer gene, and in two of
   them the therapy's own DNA was found inserted beside or inside that gene; it names two more such cases
   from other gene therapy programmes published in 2026. It says the benefits still outweigh the risks
   and that it is weighing whether to act.
   - Address: https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/safety-availability-biologics/fda-investigating-serious-risk-malignancy-linked-zolgensma-gene-therapy
   - Kind of source: regulator.
   - For or against: against, for every treatment carried into the body by the same kind of virus shell.
   - Record: no record of its own. It bears on "A person receives cell-resetting genes in a trial"
     (`a-person-receives-cell-resetting-genes-in-a-trial`) and "Gene therapy lengthens chromosome ends in
     an old animal" (`gene-therapy-lengthens-chromosome-ends-in-an-old-animal`), which use the same
     kind of carrier.
   - Held: no. The premature-ageing entry names Zolgensma only as an example of an approved gene therapy.
   - Why it matters: it is the first time a regulator has confirmed that this kind of carrier caused a
     cancer in a person, and the reprogramming and telomere gene therapies rest on it.

3. **2026-11-05. A registry study of 11,474 kidney transplant recipients found no longer life in those
   prescribed rapamycin.** The middle age at death was 64.6 years with rapamycin and 64.3 without, a
   difference the authors call statistically similar (somewhere between 1.6 years shorter and 2.0 years
   longer), over a middle follow-up of 11 years.
   - Address: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11357-026-02643-8
   - Kind of source: paper (GeroScience).
   - For or against: against, with the caution that transplant patients are not healthy older people.
   - Record: "A rapamycin-type drug improves an age-related measure in older people"
     (`rapamycin-improves-an-age-related-measure-in-older-people`).
   - Held: yes (`stewart-2026`).
   - Why it matters: it is one of the largest sets of people on rapamycin for years, and it shows no
     gain in length of life.
