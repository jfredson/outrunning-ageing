1. **2026-11-10. An antibody against tau, the second protein of Alzheimer's disease, missed its main
   outcome in a mid-stage trial.** In 466 people with early Alzheimer's followed for 80 weeks, the
   decline score worsened by 2.15 and 1.85 points on the two doses of UCB's bepranemab against 1.81 on
   placebo. The authors call the differences seen on tau scans and on one memory scale
   hypothesis-generating.
   - Address: https://doi.org/10.1016/s1474-4422(26)00357-1
   - Kind of source: paper (The Lancet Neurology).
   - For or against: against.
   - Record: no record of its own. It sits beside "An antibody clears amyloid from the brain and slows
     the decline of Alzheimer's disease" (`antibody-clears-amyloid-from-the-brain-and-slows-decline`),
     in the same branch.
   - Held: no.
   - Why it matters: clearing amyloid slows Alzheimer's a little; the hope that an antibody against
     the second protein would add to that has now failed again, with a differently aimed antibody.

2. **2026-11-09. Heart scans showed less amyloid in people given eplontersen, in the large trial that
   had missed its main outcome.** Two papers from the CARDIO-TTRansform trial of 1,432 people with
   transthyretin amyloid in the heart: in 120 people scanned by magnetic resonance, the measure of
   amyloid in the heart muscle was 6.7 percentage points lower than on placebo at week 140 (8.7 to 4.7
   lower); in 1,426 people followed by ultrasound, the heart's decline was slowed modestly, almost
   only in those not already taking a stabilising drug. Both papers repeat that the drug did not
   significantly reduce heart deaths and repeat heart events.
   - Address: https://doi.org/10.1161/circulationaha.126.083732 and
     https://doi.org/10.1161/circulationaha.126.083722
   - Kind of source: papers (Circulation).
   - For or against: for on what the scans show, against on the outcome that counts.
   - Record: "A drug stops a misfolded protein piling up in the heart and extends life"
     (`drug-stops-a-misfolded-protein-piling-up-in-the-heart`).
   - Held: the readings name eplontersen; neither paper is in the records.
   - Why it matters: it is randomised evidence that switching off the protein at its source changes
     the heart muscle itself, set against a trial in which that change did not show up as fewer deaths
     and admissions. Ionis's quarterly release of 2026-11-09 says the same of the main result
     (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/874015/000114036126042598/ef20085128_ex99-1.htm).

3. **2026-11-11. A reanalysis of 33,780 mice found that the drugs known to lengthen mouse life work

   fed mice may partly be the rescue of unhealthy animals.
   - Address: https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.11.10.765234
   - Kind of source: preprint (bioRxiv; Bouvet and Partridge).
   - For or against: against, at one remove.
   - Record: bears on "A rapamycin-type drug improves an age-related measure in older people"
     (`rapamycin-improves-an-age-related-measure-in-older-people`) and "A drug makes people live longer
     in good health" (`a-drug-adds-healthy-years-to-a-life`), both of which lean on these mouse results.
   - Held: no.
   - Why it matters: the mouse lifespan results are the main reason rapamycin, acarbose and similar
     drugs are tried in people, and this says the results may overstate what a healthy animal gains.
