# The weekly reading: how the records are read against the news

Written 2026-10-17, the day the site went live, in a cloud session from the master session's brief, as the kickoff's first after-launch step (`docs/2026-10-16-kickoff.md`, "Plan for the build", item 8). Adapted from the two siblings' documents of 2026-10-16, The Upgrade Tree's `docs/weekly-reading.md` (the closer copy source, because it shares the five-rung stage ladder) and The Quiet Galaxy's (the closer source for the publications log and the publish command), which both descend from The Belt Equation's scan plan and scan procedure (ruled by John 2026-09-19, audited 2026-10-06). The design choices in the first half were proposed, each with a confidence level and the strongest alternative, per the ground rule of 2026-09-08. **John ruled on 2026-10-17 that the schedule (Sundays at 06:00 Pacific), the caps and the widened reading record (decision 18 in `docs/record-schema.md`) are accepted as proposed**; the other choices in the table stay proposals until he rules on them. The procedure in the second half is the standalone instruction the scheduled session follows, top to bottom. The first reading ran by hand the same day (`data/scans/2026-10-17.toml`), so the site has a real reading to show; what it found, and what it could not do, is at the end.

**Changed 2026-10-18, after the first audit** (`docs/readings/audit-2026-10-18.md`; John ruled on 2026-10-18 on its proposals 1 to 9 and left 10 to 12 out). Nine changes, each marked in the step it touches: (1) the reading record carries a `limits` line saying what the reading could not see; (2) a trial registry is read through its data interface; (3) before a flagged item says the records lack something, `data/` is searched for it; (4) helper agents are allowed on conditions, and the main session opens every source behind a flagged item itself; (5) a flagged item's `sources` are pages that were opened and say the thing; (6) every release, regulator's decision or paper that was read is logged, and "read" is spelt out; (7) the window is always the seven days up to the reading; (8) the first search for a leaf is aimed at the kind of record its settler names, in its words; (9) a quiet verdict says what the searches could and could not see. The routine reads this file fresh at the start of every run, so the routine itself needs no change.

This is a working document, so it uses the project's own words (leaf, scan, settler, verdict). On the public site the reading is "This week's reading", a leaf is a treatment and the test is "what would count". The reading record's prose is printed on the public site as written, so it uses the public words, not these (John's ruling of 2026-10-18; step 1, "Written for visitors").

## The idea in one paragraph

Every leaf carries a test that says exactly what would count as it having happened, a settler that says what kind of public record settles the test, and a line in its notes or its estimate on what would move it. Every claim carries a date it can be judged by and a verdict. Every self-experiment carries a paragraph on what its subject has made public. Those are the things to read against. Once a week a fresh Claude session with no memory of earlier conversations searches the web for what happened in the past week against each one, writes down for every record what it searched, what it found and one of four verdicts, logs every paper, regulator's record, registry entry or company release that bears on a test in the publications log, moves a leaf's stage up one rung when, and only when, a record of the kind the leaf's settler names shows the next rung reached, files everything else as a flagged item for John, reruns the headline if a leaf changed, and commits. The site rebuilds from the commit. Recording the weeks when nothing happened is the point: a reader can see the records being watched instead of taking it on faith, and the publications log grows into the dated record of the science that the kickoff promised.

Three things are different from the siblings. The reading reads three kinds of record, not one: the leaves against their tests, the claims against their due dates, the self-experiments against what their subjects publish. It has a number at the top that it may not touch: the estimates the headline draws from are John's judgment, revised only with a dated reason, so the reading's room to act is narrower than The Quiet Galaxy's (which may move a range within a cap) and the same as The Upgrade Tree's (a rung, on the record the entry names). And it has a medical notice to honour: nothing the reading writes recommends anything.

## What the reading reads

- **Every tracked leaf, every week**, against the test for the rung above its current stage, with the settler saying what kind of record would count and the notes and the estimate's reasoning saying what the project expected. A leaf at the top of the ladder (`horizon = "top"`, in wide use) has no rung left and is read for a withdrawal or a recount; none is at the top as of 2026-10-17. For the five leaves shared with The Upgrade Tree, the reading also reads that site's record of the same treatment (`data/lifespan.toml` in its public repository) and writes the sibling's stage into the item, so the two sites can be seen to agree; when they do not, the item is flagged and a reading is filed on both sites.
- **Every claim, every week**, against its due year and its verdict: has the date passed with the verdict still not yet due; did the thing it commits to happen; did the speaker restate, move or withdraw it; is there a better primary source than the record holds. The reading never gives a verdict: a claim whose date has passed is a flagged item with the proposed verdict and the record it would rest on.
- **Every self-experiment, every week**, against what its subject has published: a paper, a data release, a new measurement, a new claim, an end to the programme, a change to what the public record shows. A self-experiment moves no leaf, and the reading says so again whenever one looks as if it should.
- **The `verify` lists are read, not worked.** The reading reads a record's list so that it does not report as new a fact the list already names, and so that a search which settles a listed fact either way becomes a flagged item with the finding. It may not edit or clear a list.

## What the reading may and may not do

**It may:**

- log a publication under `data/papers/`, with `added_on` the reading's date, once the paper's own page (the journal, the DOI, the registry entry, the regulator's notice, the company's own release) has been read and its date, authors, title and venue checked there; `moved_range` false; `settled_stage` true only when the same reading moved a leaf on it;
- move a leaf's stage up by exactly one rung, onto `shown-in-animals` or `first-human` only, when a primary record of the kind named in the leaf's `settler` shows the next rung's test met on a plain reading (two readers would agree), dated in the window, with the leaf's `stage`, `stage_reached_on`, `stage_record` and `stage_publication` set, the old stage moved into `earlier_stage`, and a `[[revision]]` dated the reading's day whose `why` begins "The weekly reading of YYYY-MM-DD";
- rerun the headline (`python3 scripts/compute.py run`) when a leaf changed, and commit `data/headline.json`;
- write the reading record and the changelog line, and commit and push.

**It may not:**

- edit a `test`, a `settler`, a `name`, a `description`, a `horizon`, a `mechanism`, a `breaking_point`, a `sibling`, or the `notes` of any record;
- touch an `[estimate]` in any way: not a year, not the reasoning, not the status; the estimates are the one judgment on the leaf and they are John's, revised by him with a dated reason and a source;
- move a leaf onto `in-clinical-use` or `in-wide-use`, however good the record: the headline reads the clinic rung, and a leaf arriving there changes what is drawn and needs its estimate rewritten, which is John's (design choice 4);
- move a leaf more than one rung in one reading, move a leaf down, or move one on press coverage, a company's claim without the record the settler names, a plan, a target date, a funding round, a preprint where the settler asks for a paper, or a conference presentation;
- move a stage on a record older than the window that the entry missed: that record means the entry as written was wrong, and correcting an entry is John's (The Upgrade Tree's rule of 2026-10-16, adopted here);
- change a claim's `verdict`, `verdict_on` or `verdict_why`, or any other field of a claim or a self-experiment; add, retire or rename any record; touch `data/branches.toml`, `data/retired.toml`, the headline parameters in `scripts/compute.py` or anything in `docs/`;
- edit or clear a `verify` list;
- edit a publication record that already exists, or a reading record;
- change the headline's method or parameters, or the fixed dates.

Anything it would have done outside these limits becomes a `flagged` item: the change it would have made, the record it rests on, and why it was not the reading's to make. John rules after the fact by editing the record himself, with a revision or a dated note that names the reading's date; he never edits the reading.

## The four verdicts

| Verdict | Meaning | What the item carries |
|---|---|---|
| `quiet` | Nothing in the window bears on the test. `found` still says in a sentence what was seen and why it falls short. | the searches and `found` |
| `noted` | Something in the window bears on the test and does not meet it, and it was logged in the publications log. A reading never says "something relevant happened" without logging it, so the log is the one place the science accumulates. | `sources`, `publications` (at least one, which must exist) |
| `moved` | A leaf only. The next rung's test is met on a primary record of the kind the settler names, dated in the window; the leaf is moved one rung, onto `shown-in-animals` or `first-human`, with a stage revision dated the reading's day. | `sources`, `moved_from`, `moved_to`, usually `publications` |
| `flagged` | Anything the reading may not decide on its own, with `for_john` saying the change it would have made, the record, and why it stopped: an older record the entry missed, a press-only report, a record it could not open to check, a move onto the clinic rung, a claim past its due year, a claim restated or withdrawn, a self-experiment ended or contradicted by the record, a settled `verify` fact, a disagreeing sibling stage, a test that no longer fits what was found, a validation error. | `sources`, `for_john`, and `publications` where one was logged |

The record layout of 2026-10-16 had five verdicts for a leaf-only reading (quiet, noted, moved, met, waiting). Four replace them (design choice 2): `met` folds into `moved`, because the reading may not touch an estimate, so a move can only ever be a rung; `waiting` becomes `flagged`, the siblings' word, and carries its line for John.

## The caps (ruled by John 2026-10-17, as proposed)

1. **One rung per leaf per reading, and only onto `shown-in-animals` or `first-human`.** A move onto the clinic rung is flagged with the record.
2. **No more than three leaves moved in one reading.** A fourth move is flagged with the record and the words "over the cap"; a week that moves four leaves is a week John should read himself before the site says so.
3. **Two to four searches aimed at each record**, and no more than four, with a borrowed search listed under the record it was borrowed for and a `reused` line saying why. A search aimed at another record never counts toward the floor of two. If the search tool fails twice, or refuses because its allowance is spent, the item carries `search_short` saying so and the floor is waived for that item.
4. **No more than three publications logged per item**, the ones that bear most directly on the test; the rest are named in `found`.
5. **A publication is logged only after its page has been read.** A paper, release or registry entry seen only in a search result goes into the flagged item's `for_john` with its citation, for the next reading to log once it can be opened.

## The reading record

One file per run, `data/scans/YYYY-MM-DD.toml` (`-2`, `-3` for a second or third run the same day), written once and never edited. The kickoff named the folder `data/scans/` and the record layout, the validator and the site followed it on 2026-10-16, so it stays, although the siblings' folder is `data/readings/`; the record layout says so. The layout is in `docs/record-schema.md` under "The scan record", and `scripts/compute.py validate` refuses the site a build if a record breaks it. In short: a header with the date, the first day of the window, the time it finished, who ran it (`hand` or `scheduled`), the commit the records were read at, how many leaves, claims and self-experiments were read and how many distinct searches were run, and a summary paragraph; then one `[[item]]` per record read, in the order read, naming exactly one leaf, claim or self-experiment, with the searches, what was found in one to three plain sentences, the verdict, the sources for every verdict but quiet, the publications logged, the rungs for a move, the line for John on a flagged item, and the sibling's stage on a shared leaf. The validator checks that the header counts match the items, that every item lists two searches or says why not, that a noted item names a publication that exists, that a moved item is a leaf with a stage revision dated the reading's day from the rung it names to the next, and that only a flagged item carries a line for John.

## What the reader sees

Three surfaces, the tracker pattern's section 5 (ruled by John 2026-10-16):

- **The strip on the home page**, under the year: "Last checked 17 October 2026: 44 treatments, 27 claims and 9 self-experiments read; none moved; 19 left for the author", with the moved treatment and "the week's reading" as links.
- **"This week's reading"** at `/reading/`, in the top bar: the latest reading at the top with its summary and its counts, every item grouped by branch (a claim or a self-experiment under the first branch it bears on; the claims about the headline itself under "the whole question"), under the summary, any dated note added by a later batch (`later_note`) and the reading's `limits` line; each item a card with the verdict as a badge, the found sentence, the line for the author where there is one, and the searches and sources folded away; the flagged items still waiting on John; earlier readings collapsed below; and the key to the four verdicts.
- **On each treatment's page**, its last three readings, each a line with the date, the verdict, the found sentence and the sources. The claims and self-experiments pages do not yet show theirs; the export carries them for when they do.

## What the first reading found

Run by hand on 2026-10-17 in the cloud session that built this document, with twelve searchers each given a branch of leaves, ten claims or the self-experiments, and their findings read and assembled by the session into `data/scans/2026-10-17.toml`. Two limits shaped it, and both are written in the record: the session's network policy refused every page outside the search tool (journals, DOIs, registries, regulators, company newsrooms), so no citation could be checked and, by cap 5, nothing was logged; and the search tool's allowance of 200 searches per turn, shared across the twelve searchers, ran out before the end, so 12 records had fewer than two searches of their own and three claims were not read at all. The scheduled run will have the tool to itself and should be given network access to the hosts the records name (`docs/weekly-reading-prompt.md`). The nineteen flagged items, nearly all a record older than the week that the entry as written did not know, are on the reading page under "Waiting on the author" and in the pull request that delivered this document; the twelve citations they carry are for the next reading to log.

The same day, 2026-10-17, John ruled that the schedule, the caps and the widened record are accepted as proposed and asked for the nineteen flagged items to be applied as a dated batch, moving nothing onto the clinic rung without him. ## The procedure

The standalone instruction for the scheduled reading. A fresh Claude session with no memory of any earlier conversation follows this from top to bottom. When this file and anything else disagree, this file wins for the reading and the disagreement is a flag for John.

### 0. Orient

- Get the date in John's time zone before anything else: `TZ=America/Los_Angeles date +%F`. That is the reading's `read_on` and its id; every date you write is absolute (year-month-day), never "today" or "last week".
- The repository `jfredson/outrunning-ageing` is cloned in the cloud workspace; `cd` into it. Never work in the linked computer's copy at `~/Code/outrunning-ageing`; John's own sessions run there.
- Switch to main and bring it up to date with GitHub in one command: `git fetch origin && git checkout -B main origin/main`. The workspace clones onto a session branch named `claude/...` and its copy of main can be days old; the publish command refuses to push from any branch but main and refuses a main that is behind. Nothing in a fresh clone is worth keeping.
- Record `git rev-parse --short HEAD` as `commit_before`.
- Read this file, `docs/record-schema.md` (the scan record, the publication record, the leaf record's stage fields and revision log), `CLAUDE.md` at the root (plain language; absolute dates; no internal vocabulary on public pages; nothing about John's own health; a commit hook that refuses a relative date), and `docs/weekly-reading-prompt.md`.
- The window is the seven days up to the reading: `covers_from` is seven days before `read_on` (a reading on 2026-10-25 covers from 2026-10-18), whatever the date of the last reading, so that a missed week or a short gap leaves no hole; say in the summary that it overlaps the last reading, and that a record the last reading already logged is seen again and named as already logged. Read the newest file in `data/scans/` all the same, for what its summary says it could not read, and read those records first.
- Load the records with Python's `tomllib` to get the lists: every file under `data/leaves/`, `data/claims/` and `data/experiments/`. Do not parse by hand. Every record is read every week; a record the last reading could not read (its summary names them) is read first.
- Load The Upgrade Tree's `data/lifespan.toml` from its public repository (`https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jfredson/upgrade-tree/main/data/lifespan.toml`) for the five shared leaves' sibling stages; if it cannot be reached, say so in the summary and leave the sibling fields off.

### 1. Read each record against the world

**Helpers (allowed, on conditions).** The reading may be shared among helper agents, one group of records each, as the first two readings were. Every helper gets the same brief, which is this step and the caps, and writes only its own output file; none edits the repository or runs git. The main session reads every flagged item itself and, before it files one, opens each source the item cites (or says in the item that the source was not opened and why). The main session names the helpers, the groups and the brief in the header comment of the reading record, and says in `limits` that helpers were used.

For each record, the leaves first in branch order (`data/branches.toml`, `order` 1 to 8) and by id within a branch, then the claims by id, then the self-experiments by id:

1. Compose two to four web searches from the record's test: a leaf's `test` for the rung above its stage, the kind of record its `settler` names, and the names in its `notes` (a drug, a trial, a company); a claim's `who`, `commits_to` and `due`; a self-experiment's `who`, `name` and `measures`. Two is a floor: at least one aimed at the kind of record the settler names (a journal, a regulator's register, a trial registry, a company's own newsroom) and at least one at wider reporting, worded differently, both listed in the item's `queries`. For a leaf, put the kind of record in the words of the first query ("ClinicalTrials.gov", "FDA approval", the journal's name, the company's name with "press release"), so that anyone reading the record can see the search was aimed there; the validator cannot check this and the audit samples it. Ask for the window in the words (the month, "this week"); the search tool cannot limit results by date, so this only asks, and `limits` says so. A search counts toward a record only if it was aimed at it; a search first run for another record may be listed again under this one with a `reused` line quoting it as written, naming the record it was first run for, and saying in one line why it bears on this record's test; a borrowed search never counts toward the floor. Read the record's `verify` list first, so that a fact it already names is not reported as new.
2. Read the results that bear on the test, literally: two people reading the test would agree whether the thing named has happened. A press claim, a plan, a target date, a funding round, a demonstration, a conference presentation, a preprint where the settler asks for a paper, and a company's statement that a patient has been dosed are not the record unless the settler says the company's own release counts. Open the record's own page (the journal, the DOI, the registry entry, the regulator's notice, the release) and check its date, authors, title and venue there before logging it or moving anything on it. Some pages need a different road. For a trial registry number, open `https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT…` (the ordinary study page is an empty shell to a page-reading tool; the data interface gives status, enrolment, dates and any posted results as text). For a paper whose journal page asks for a sign-in, Europe PMC gives the abstract, authors and date (`https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI:%22…%22&format=json&resultType=core`) and, for an open copy, the full text (`…/rest/PMC…/fullTextXML`); Crossref (`https://api.crossref.org/works/<DOI>`) gives a title, authors and date. A company's release that its own site refuses can be read as the wire text on a news site that carries it; say so in the publication's `notes`. A PDF that comes back as unreadable bytes is read with a PDF reader on the machine, not skipped.
3. Give one verdict (the table above). For `moved`, name the rung and the record. For `flagged`, write `for_john`: the change you would have made, the record, and why it is not yours; when the record could not be opened, say so and give the citation as seen, for the next reading to log.
   - **Written for visitors.** `found`, `for_john`, `search_short`, `reused`, the `summary`, the `limits` line, a publication's `finding` and `notes`, and a revision's `why` are printed on the public pages as written, so they follow "Writing for visitors" in `docs/record-schema.md` (ruled by John 2026-10-18): plain words, no file, field or record ids (name a treatment, a claim, a self-experiment or a paper in words: "the 2019 trial report by Hickson and colleagues", not `hickson-2019`), no ladder keys ("first human", not `first-human`), no internal names ("leaf", "settler", "session", "batch", "verify list"), and no numbered references to documents a visitor cannot see. The `queries` are the exception: they are logged exactly as run.
   - **Look in the records first.** Before a flagged item says a record is missing something ("the notes do not mention", "not in the log"), search `data/` for it: the registry number, the distinctive words of the title, the company's or the first author's name, across `data/papers/`, `data/leaves/`, `data/claims/`, `data/experiments/`, `CHANGELOG.md` and the newest earlier reading. Write in `found` what was searched. A fact the records already hold is not new: the item is `quiet` and names where it is held.
   - **Sources that carry the fact.** The `sources` of a flagged item are pages you opened and that say what the item says it says. A page seen only in a search result goes in `for_john` as "seen in a search result only", with its citation. Say in `for_john` which sources were opened and which were not.
   - **What a quiet sentence covers.** The `found` sentence of a quiet item names at least one thing the searches returned (a title, a date, a company) or says that no result turned up in a news-style search for the window. It never says that nothing happened: the search tool cannot limit results by date, so it can show only what it returned.

Search economy: no fewer than two searches aimed at each record, no more than four. If the search tool fails twice, or refuses because its allowance is spent, write `search_short` on the item saying so, give the verdict the searches you have support, and move on; a record with no search at all is left out of the reading and named in the summary, to be read first at the next reading.

### 2. Log the publications

For every paper, regulator's record, registry entry or company release that a `noted`, `moved` or `flagged` item rests on, and whose own page you have read, add a publication record under `data/papers/` (unless that publication is already there, which the item says) in the layout `docs/record-schema.md` gives: id from the first author's surname and year (a company's name for a release; a word added when two collide, as `hickson-2019-senolytics` would), `date` the record's own date as text, `added_on` the reading's date, `authors` as printed (the first twelve and a count when there are more), `title`, `venue` with volume and pages where they exist, `link`, a `finding` of two to four plain sentences with the numbers that matter, the `leaves` and `branches` it bears on, `moved_range = false`, `settled_stage = false` (true only when step 3 makes it a stage record), a `verify` list for anything on the page you could not confirm (or, better, leave it out), and a `notes` line naming the reading in words ("Logged by the weekly reading of 2026-10-25."). No more than three per item. "Read" means opened and read, and it includes a company's own release (read as wire text if its page refuses), a regulator's decision, a registry entry read through its data interface and an open copy of a paper; a page that asks for a sign-in is logged from the abstract, with a `notes` line saying so. A record is not left unlogged because it is dated before the window: the window decides what is news, not what is logged. Never edit a publication record that already exists; if one is wrong, say so in the report. Check each file with `python3 -c "import tomllib,sys; tomllib.load(open(sys.argv[1],'rb'))" data/papers/X.toml` and then `python3 scripts/compute.py validate`.

### Standing rules

- Plain language, UK spelling, absolute dates, commas rather than dashes, and no bare ids. In the reading record's printed lines (everything but `queries`) there are no ids at all: a treatment, a claim, a self-experiment and a publication are named in words, as "Writing for visitors" in `docs/record-schema.md` says (ruled by John 2026-10-18). In the report to John, the id goes beside the words.
- Read, do not infer: a rung moves on a record about its test, not on mood about the field. A self-experiment moves no leaf. A claim is judged by John.
- One reading, one commit, made by the publish command. Never force-push, never rebase, never touch a branch other than main and the one fallback branch.
- If anything in this file cannot be followed as written, do the parts that can, flag the rest for John in the report, and never improvise an edit outside the limits above.

## Deferred, and why

- **Readings shown on the claims and self-experiments pages.** The export carries each record's last three readings; the pages show the treatments' only, until the first audit says the others earn the space.
- **Reader-submitted items** ("did this move anything?"): a GitHub issue through the disagree page, answered by a reading item. No new machinery.
- **Searching beyond the open web** (journal feeds, the registries' own interfaces): the settlers name their records and a web search reaches most of them; add a per-source fetch only once a reading has demonstrably missed something a feed would have caught.
- **The reading proposing new leaves**: it may flag a gap; adding a leaf is John's and the yearly review's.
- **The sibling check filing a reading on The Upgrade Tree.** The item records the sibling's stage and flags a disagreement; filing on the other site needs that repository attached to the run, which is a change to the routine John makes once a disagreement has happened.
