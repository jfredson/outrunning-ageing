## The question

Every year you live, medicine gets a little better. Right now it does not get better fast enough: for a person of forty, the expected years left fall by roughly a year every year, give or take the small gains that new treatments add. Longevity escape velocity is the name for the moment that stops being true: the point when medicine adds more than a year of healthy life for every year that passes, so that a person who keeps up with the best available care finds their remaining life expectancy holding steady or rising rather than falling. The phrase was coined by David Gobel and made famous by the biogerontologist Aubrey de Grey around 2004, borrowing the rocket's idea of a speed at which you stop falling back.

The site asks: **when, if ever, does the rate of repair overtake the rate of ageing?** It does for that question what The Belt Equation does for a settled solar system: it breaks a question people file under science fiction, or under marketing, into pieces anyone can argue about, with a number at the top that is computed from the pieces and never chosen by hand.

Three things the project is for, in John's words from the brief: all the scientific work toward human longevity, tracked; the self-experiments others run in public (Bryan Johnson's is the famous one); and his own research and experimentation. The first two are the public site. The third stays private in version one, by his ruling.

How this differs from The Upgrade Tree, which already has a lifespan-and-repair branch: The Upgrade Tree asks what we can change on purpose today and counts stages reached. This site asks when, which needs a date and a probability, and a tree built around the mechanisms of ageing rather than the capacities of a person. The five lifespan entries The Upgrade Tree holds today (a drug adds healthy years, partial reprogramming reverses ageing in an animal, a drug clears aged cells from a person, a gene-edited pig organ, a lab-grown organ) each have a matching leaf here, and the two records point at each other (see "How it is built", item 7).

## The headline number

Proposed form, two numbers on the first screen and one beside them:

1. **The year.** The middle estimate of the year longevity escape velocity arrives under the project's current ranges, shown with its spread: "2063, and anywhere from 2044 to not this century." This is the thing the reader holds at a glance, the way The Belt Equation's reader holds a percentage and The Quiet Galaxy's reader holds a count.
2. **The chance it arrives before 2050.** A fixed, stated date, chosen because the loudest promises on the claims log (Ray Kurzweil's 2029, Aubrey de Grey's "a fifty-fifty chance within about seventeen years of the first rejuvenated mouse") all land before it, and because it is a date most readers under fifty expect to see. The date is a project constant, changed only at a yearly review and logged when it changes.
3. **Beside them, the share of runs in which it does not arrive before 2100.** Counted in public as "not this century". This is the honest width of the argument, the number most people have never seen, and the counterpart of The Quiet Galaxy's optimist-to-pessimist spread.

**How it is computed from the leaves, and never typed in.** Every leaf (a specific intervention or measurement tool, see below) sits on a stage ladder and carries the project's estimate of when it reaches each remaining rung, as a range: earliest plausible year, most likely year, latest plausible year, with the reasoning and sources written beside it. The compute script runs the tree many thousands of times. In each run it draws a year for every leaf's arrival at "in clinical use" from its range, works out for each branch the year that branch counts as handled (a stated number of its leaves in clinical use, a field on the branch, default two), takes the latest handled year across the branches marked required, and adds a drawn lag for the treatments to spread from the clinic to ordinary people. The year that comes out is that run's escape year. The middle of all the runs is the headline year; the share of runs landing before 2050 is the second number; the share landing after 2100 is the third. This is the Belt Equation's mechanism (play the tree out, read the result) with The Quiet Galaxy's habit of drawing from ranges rather than fixed values.

Confidence: moderate on the form, which is a judgment call; high that it must be computed from the leaves rather than chosen, which is the family rule (tracker pattern, rule 1.1). Strongest alternative: The Belt Equation's framing, the chance it arrives before John's own actuarial life-expectancy date, with the year dial. It is a stronger story and the Belt Equation already uses it. Against: it ties the public number to John's own life just after he ruled his own record private, and his actuarial date is already on the Belt Equation for anyone who wants it. Keep it as a version-two option alongside a "your odds" page with a birth-year control. Second alternative: no year at all, only a count of interventions in clinical use per branch, which is what The Upgrade Tree does. That would make the two sites the same site.

Two honesty rules on the model, stated on the method page: the combination rule (all required branches handled, plus a lag) is the project's judgment and the strongest alternative, a weighted sum in which some branches matter more, is recorded there with why it was not chosen; and the ranges are the project's reading of the evidence, revised only with a dated reason and a source, never because a number looked wrong.

## The branches

Between four and nine, fixed, changed only at a yearly review (tracker pattern, rule 2.1). The published science groups ageing into "hallmarks": nine in the 2013 paper by Carlos López-Otín and colleagues, twelve in their 2023 update. Twelve is too many to hold after one visit, and several are hard to tell apart without training. Aubrey de Grey's seven "damage classes" are cleaner but are one school's framing. Proposed: eight branches, seven kinds of damage that repair would have to address, grouped from the twelve hallmarks, and one branch for proving that anything works. Each is a reason the headline reads as it does: a branch with no treatment in the clinic pushes the year out; a branch in which clocks are accepted as trial endpoints pulls it in.

| key | plain name | what it covers (and the hallmarks it groups) | example leaves |
|---|---|---|---|
| `worn-out-cells` | Worn-out cells | Cells that have stopped dividing but will not die, and poison their neighbours (cellular senescence). | A drug clears aged cells from a person; senolytics in knee and lung trials; the 2020 failure of Unity's first trial. |
| `damaged-dna` | Damaged DNA | Mutations and shortened chromosome ends (genomic instability, telomere attrition). | Gene therapy for a single ageing-related mutation; telomerase therapy in a living animal. |
| `cell-settings` | The cell's settings | The marks that tell a cell which genes to read, which drift with age (epigenetic alterations). | Partially resetting cells reverses ageing in a living animal; a first person dosed with a reprogramming therapy. |
| `broken-proteins` | Broken proteins and waste | Misfolded proteins, failed recycling, waste that builds up inside and between cells (loss of proteostasis, disabled autophagy, cross-links). | Drugs that clear amyloid in the body as a whole; autophagy-boosting drugs with a human result. |
| `energy-and-fuel` | Energy and fuel sensing | The cell's power plants and the pathways that sense food (mitochondrial dysfunction, deregulated nutrient sensing). | A drug makes people live longer in good health (rapamycin, metformin and their successors); the TAME trial enrolling. |
| `replacing-parts` | Replacing lost cells and parts | Stem cells that run out, tissue that is not replaced, organs that fail (stem cell exhaustion, and the regenerative and replacement route). | A pig organ keeps a person alive; a lab-grown organ works in a person for a year; a stem cell therapy restores a function in a controlled trial. |
| `inflammation-and-signalling` | Inflammation and the body's messaging | Low-grade chronic inflammation, the gut's microbes, and the signals cells send each other (chronic inflammation, dysbiosis, altered intercellular communication). | Plasma exchange or dilution with a measured effect in people; an anti-inflammatory with a healthy-years result. |
| `measuring-age` | Measuring ageing | The tools that say whether any of the above worked without waiting decades: biological age clocks, blood and imaging markers, and whether a regulator will accept one as proof. | A clock validated to predict outcomes in a large cohort; a regulator accepting an ageing marker as a trial endpoint; the FDA recognising ageing as a treatable indication. |

The measuring branch is marked required: without an accepted way to show that a treatment slowed ageing, every other branch's path from "shown in animals" to "in clinical use" runs through a trial that takes a working lifetime, and the model should say so.

Confidence: moderate. The grouping is a judgment call; the mapping from each of the twelve hallmarks to a branch is written in the record schema so that a reader can check nothing was dropped. Strongest alternative: the twelve hallmarks as twelve branches, which is the published taxonomy and needs no defence, at the cost of three over the family's limit and a first screen no one can hold. Second alternative: de Grey's seven damage classes, which were designed for exactly this question, at the cost of adopting one camp's map in a field where the camps disagree.

## What a leaf is

A leaf is one specific intervention, result or tool that would have to arrive for the headline to move: a drug, a procedure, a gene therapy, a measurement accepted by a regulator. It has:

- **A plain name** that says what happens, not what it is called ("A drug clears aged cells from a person", not "senolytic therapy").
- **A test**: what would count, written so that two readers agree. "A peer-reviewed, controlled trial in people reports that those taking the drug stayed free of age-related disease longer than those on placebo, by the margin the trial was designed to detect." Swallowing the pill is not the leaf; the result is.
- **A settler**: who or what settles the test. The peer-reviewed report, the regulator's approval record, the trial registry.
- **A stage**, on the same five-rung ladder The Upgrade Tree uses, with the same keys so the two records can point at each other: idea only, shown in animals, first human, in clinical use, in wide use. The stage never moves down; a withdrawal is a status and a note. Each stage reached carries a date and a public record.
- **The project's estimate** of the year each remaining rung is reached, as a range (earliest, likely, latest) with the reasoning and the sources in a sentence or two. This is what the compute script draws from, and it is the one thing on the leaf that is a judgment rather than a record.
- **A revision log**: every change to the range, dated, with its reason and source.
- **Cross-links**: the claims that promise this leaf, the papers behind its stage and its range, and, where one exists, the matching Upgrade Tree entry.
- **A verify list**: anything written from memory, until it is checked against a source. Never presented as settled.

About forty leaves in version one, five per branch on average, more under the branches with the most activity (energy and fuel sensing, worn-out cells, replacing parts) and fewer under damaged DNA.

## Six kinds of record

The data is the project. Six record kinds, each in a plain text format that reads well in a diff, the same TOML layout as the siblings:

1. **Branches** (`data/branches.toml`). The eight branches in order: key, plain name, one sentence of meaning, which hallmarks it groups, whether it is required, how many leaves in clinical use count as handled.
2. **Leaves** (`data/<branch>.toml`, one file per branch). As described above.
3. **The claims log** (`data/claims/`). Who said what about when, with the primary source: Aubrey de Grey's fifty-fifty-within-about-seventeen-years and his "the first thousand-year-old is probably less than twenty years younger than the first hundred-and-fifty-year-old"; Ray Kurzweil's escape velocity by 2029; David Sinclair's statements on age reversal in people; Bryan Johnson's targets; the stated aims and funding of Altos Labs, Retro Biosciences, Calico, Unity, Hevolution and the XPRIZE Healthspan. Each entry: who, what they said, when, where (a primary source, not a press summary), what date or result it commits to, which leaf or branch it bears on, and a verdict column filled in as dates pass (delivered, missed, not yet due, unfalsifiable). The log sits beside the headline and moves no number; the tracker pattern's rule for talk set against delivery (section 6).
4. **Self-experiments** (`data/experiments/`). Public self-experiments other people run: who, what they do, what they measure, what they claim, what the public record shows, and the one line the site repeats everywhere: a claim about one person is a story, not a record, and it moves no leaf. Bryan Johnson's Blueprint, the Rejuvenation Olympics leaderboard, and the handful of published single-person studies. John's own track is not in this record in version one, by his ruling; when he publishes a piece of it, it enters here under the same rules as everyone else's.
5. **The publications log** (`data/papers/`). Append-only and dated: the founding papers (the hallmarks papers of 2013 and 2023; the 2009 rapamycin result in old mice; the 2011 and 2016 senescent-cell clearance papers; the 2016 partial reprogramming paper and the 2020 optic nerve paper; Steve Horvath's 2013 clock; the 2024 argument that radical life extension is implausible on current trends), the trial record (Unity's 2020 failure, the TAME trial's funding history, the Dog Aging Project, the 2025 rapamycin trial in healthy adults, Life Biosciences dosing a first person in June 2026), and from launch onward every paper the weekly reading turns up. Each entry says which leaf or branch it bears on and whether it moved a range.
6. **The weekly reading** (`data/scans/`). Every leaf read against the week's news, with a verdict per leaf and the held-steady entries that are the site's credibility: "a company announced a first dose; the test asks for a measured result; nothing moved."

The links between the six are what make the site useful: a leaf page lists the claims that promise it, the papers behind it and the self-experiments that try it; a claim page shows the leaf it rests on and how far that leaf has got; a branch page shows what would move it.

## How it is built

Proposed, with confidence and the strongest alternative, per the ground rule The Belt Equation set on 2026-09-08.

1. **Same shape as the siblings: data in TOML, Python standard-library scripts, an Astro static site in `site/`, deployed to Cloudflare Workers static assets, a GitHub Action deploying on every push to main, two repository secrets John adds himself.** High confidence: three sites run this way and the publish script, the deploy workflow and the changelog discipline are already debugged. The Upgrade Tree is the closest copy source because it shares the stage ladder. Alternative: a section of the Sentient Horizons site. Cheaper, but the project is its own thing with its own address, like every sibling.
2. **Join the Sentient Horizons design standard from day one:** the shared skeleton (`shell.css`) plus a skin of this site's own, which the family design review of 2026-10-16 has already begun to direct, with the crossing chart as its trunk object; the top bar and footer from `shell.html`, the parent link in the footer, every colour a token. High confidence; standard practice now the standard exists. Public pages follow the standard's writing rule: no project shorthand, every idea explained before it is named, and "hallmark", "senolytic", "epigenetic" each get their plain meaning first.
3. **The seven pages every tracker has, under the ruled public names** (tracker pattern, section 4): the site's name (home: eyebrow, the year, the question, one paragraph, the last-checked strip, two buttons); "The tree" with one page per branch carrying the six things a branch page carries; one page per leaf; "The story so far" (append-only, six kinds of entry, held-steady with equal billing); "This week's reading"; "How the numbers are worked out", with the model and its alternatives written out and the working documents linked with prefaces; "Disagree with something", with three issue templates (a missing leaf with its test; a questioned test or settler; an argued range). Plus the two optional pages this site is built around, in the heavier tier of the top bar: the claims log and the self-experiments. High confidence; ruled for the family on 2026-10-16.
4. **The compute script runs the model as a spread** (see "The headline number") and writes one computed file per build; the export script is the only reader of the data, so the site can never show two numbers that disagree. High confidence on the one-reader rule (family rule 1.5). Moderate on the model. Alternative: hand-set probabilities per leaf with no year ranges, as The Belt Equation's events carry. Ranges are easier to argue about in public than a bare probability, and this question is about a date.
5. **The validator refuses** a leaf without a test, a settler or a stage record; a range whose earliest year is after its likely year; a claim without a primary source; a self-experiment entry that moves a leaf. `python3 scripts/compute.py --check` passes before any commit that touches `data/`. High confidence; same as the siblings.
6. **The stage ladder is The Upgrade Tree's, keys and all.** High confidence: it exists, it was ruled on 2026-10-16, and sharing it is what makes the cross-links readable. Alternative: a ladder of the project's own with a "phase 3 trial" rung. The extra rung is useful; propose it to both sites at the yearly review rather than fork the ladder now.
7. **The two records for the shared lifespan leaves.** Each site keeps its own record (The Upgrade Tree's headline count depends on its five entries, and this site's ranges have no place there). Each entry carries a `sibling` field naming the matching id on the other site, and the weekly reading checks that the stages agree and files a held-steady or a move on both when they do not. Moderate confidence; a judgment call. Alternative: one source record feeding both sites through their export scripts. Cleaner in theory, more machinery, and it makes one repository depend on another at build time, which no sibling does today.
8. **UK spelling ("ageing"), as The Upgrade Tree's record already uses.** High confidence; consistency inside the family beats either spelling's merits. The US spelling of the domain, if a name with the word is chosen, is bought as a forwarding address.
9. **A one-line medical notice on every page's footer:** the site records what has been shown and what has been promised; it recommends nothing, and nothing on it is medical advice. High confidence. This is new to the family because the subject is new; the siblings have no reader who might act on a page by taking a drug.
10. **Licences from the first commit:** Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 for the data, MIT for the scripts and the site. Settled by the design standard.
11. **No analytics, no comments, no newsletter in version one.** Moderate confidence, same reasoning as the siblings.
12. **Nothing from John's private record in the repository or the site**, and no field in the schema for it, so that it cannot arrive by accident. When he rules a piece public, it enters the self-experiments record under the same rules as everyone else's, by a dated entry. High confidence; this is his ruling of 2026-10-16 made structural.

## Access, as a position the project takes

John asked whether a public project of this kind could earn access to better treatments and information. The position, carried from the planning session's answer and taken here as the project's stance rather than a promise:

Public tracking buys credibility and conversations, not treatments. Bryan Johnson's access is bought; Peter Attia's comes from a medical licence and a large audience. The one free route to anything experimental is trial participation, and for John that route runs through active duty, the eligibility rules of the SERE pipeline, and the open angioedema workup, with anything experimental a flight-surgeon conversation first. Direct-to-consumer genetic testing is parked pending the Department of Defense policy check already on the private Longevity project. So the project treats access as a possible dividend of doing the public work well, never as its thesis, and the site says nothing about what John takes or does unless he rules a piece public. The thesis is the number, and whether the people promising it deliver.
