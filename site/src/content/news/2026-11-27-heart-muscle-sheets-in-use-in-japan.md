Sheets of heart muscle grown from stem cells are now being given to patients with failing hearts in Japan, outside any trial. Japan approved the product, called RiHEART and made by the company [Cuorips](https://cuorips.co.jp/en/), on March 6th, 2026, and public health insurance has paid for it since September 1st ([Cuorips' notice of October 1st, 2026](https://cuorips.co.jp/news-en/200261001)). The first operation under that cover was done at [Osaka Police Hospital](https://www.oph.gr.jp/) on September 29th. So I have moved my entry for [heart muscle grown from stem cells and grafted onto a failing heart](/leaves/heart-muscle-grown-from-stem-cells-is-grafted-onto-a-failing-heart/) from tried in people to in use with patients.

## What the treatment is

A heart attack kills part of the heart's muscle, and the heart does not grow it back. The heart that is left has to work harder, and many people go on to [heart failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_failure), in which the heart cannot pump enough blood. RiHEART is thin sheets of beating heart muscle cells, grown in a laboratory from donor cells that were first turned back into stem cells ([induced pluripotent stem cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_pluripotent_stem_cell)). A surgeon lays the sheets on the outside of the damaged heart. The approval covers people with severe heart failure after heart attacks whom drugs and other standard treatment have not helped enough ([the review report](https://www.pmda.go.jp/files/000281693.pdf) of the [Pharmaceuticals and Medical Devices Agency](https://www.pmda.go.jp/english/), Japan's regulator).

## Why a conditional approval counts

Japan gave the approval on conditions and for seven years. Within that time the company must follow every patient treated and come back with enough evidence for a full approval ([the review report](https://www.pmda.go.jp/files/000281693.pdf); [the regulator's list of approved products](https://www.pmda.go.jp/files/000283257.pdf)). The test for this step on my site asks that a regulator approve such a graft for heart failure. A conditional approval counts when, under it, doctors can give the treatment outside a trial, and the same rule applies to every treatment I track. RiHEART meets it, because it is being paid for by public insurance and given as ordinary care.

The same rule kept a second Japanese approval of the same day off the list. Dopamine-making cells for Parkinson's disease, grown from stem cells in the same way, were approved on the same conditions. But for now their maker supplies them only to hospitals running the study it must run after approval, so they are not yet in use outside a trial ([the entry on cells grown from stem cells](/leaves/cells-grown-from-stem-cells-restore-a-lost-organ-function/)).

## The case against reading much into it

The approval rests on very little. The study behind it treated 8 patients at five hospitals between 2019 and 2024, with no comparison group ([the review report](https://www.pmda.go.jp/files/000281693.pdf)). And Japan has done this before. In 2015 it gave the same kind of approval to HeartSheet, sheets made from a patient's own muscle cells by the company [Terumo](https://www.terumo.com/). In July 2024 the regulator refused HeartSheet full approval, because the studies run after the conditional approval had not shown that it worked ([the regulator's record](https://www.pmda.go.jp/files/000272880.pdf)). If RiHEART is refused full approval too, my entry will say that it was withdrawn.

The heart patches from the [University Medical Center Göttingen](https://www.umg.eu/en/), which this entry described until now, are a different product and are still in trials. A small trial with no comparison group reported in May 2026 that the heart's pumping was better three months after the highest dose, and three of its 20 patients died during the study ([the trial report](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2513525)).

## What it changes, and what it does not

This makes 7 of the 45 treatments I track in use with patients, up from 6 ([the treatment's page](/leaves/heart-muscle-grown-from-stem-cells-is-grafted-onto-a-failing-heart/)). I sort the treatments into eight groups, and a group counts as dealt with in the year its second treatment reaches patients. This treatment belongs to the group for replacing lost cells and parts, whose first treatment in use is cartilage grown from a patient's own cells. That group now counts from 2026, instead of around 2032.

My estimate for the year medicine starts outrunning ageing stays at 2058, and the chance of a year before 2050 went from 4.27 to 4.31 per cent ([how the numbers are worked out](/about/#method)). The year barely moved because it cannot come until the slowest groups are dealt with, such as worn-out cells and damaged DNA, which I estimate around 2043 and 2044, and replacing lost cells and parts was not one of them.
