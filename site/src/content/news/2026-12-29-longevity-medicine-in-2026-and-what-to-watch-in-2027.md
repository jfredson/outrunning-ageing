On March 6th, 2026, Japan approved two treatments made from reprogrammed stem cells: dopamine-making nerve cells for [Parkinson's disease](https://www.sumitomo-pharma.com/news/20260306.html), and sheets of heart muscle for [hearts failing after a heart attack](https://cuorips.co.jp/news-en/20260306). Each maker calls its product the world's first of its kind. If medicine is ever to outrun ageing, it will need to replace parts of the body that wear out, and 2026 was the year a regulator first let such a treatment be sold.

## Replacement parts, approved in Japan

Both treatments start from [induced pluripotent stem cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_pluripotent_stem_cell), adult cells from a donor that are turned back into cells able to become almost any tissue, and then grown into the kind of cell a patient has lost. [Sumitomo Pharma](https://www.sumitomo-pharma.com/)'s cells are for people with Parkinson's disease whose movement problems the usual drugs no longer control well enough ([the company's announcement](https://www.sumitomo-pharma.com/news/20260306.html)). [Cuorips](https://www.cuorips.co.jp/)'s sheets are for people with severe heart failure who have run out of other treatments ([the company's announcement](https://cuorips.co.jp/news-en/20260306)). Both approvals are conditional and time-limited: the treatments can be sold, but the makers must go on showing that they work to keep them on the market.

The two have since taken different paths. Japan's public health insurance began covering the heart sheets on September 1st, 2026, and the first operation under that cover was done at a hospital in Osaka on September 29th ([Cuorips's notice](https://www.cuorips.co.jp/news-en/200261001)). The Parkinson's cells were first given after approval in October 2026, at [Kyoto University Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University_Hospital), but inside the study that the approval requires; Sumitomo Pharma plans about ten more patients in its next financial year and a wider rollout only after that study ends ([its results presentation of October 30th](https://www.sumitomo-pharma.com/news/assets/pdf/eir20261030.pdf)).

I have not yet decided whether either counts on this site as [in use with patients](/leaves/cells-grown-from-stem-cells-restore-a-lost-organ-function/). The test for [the heart grafts](/leaves/heart-muscle-grown-from-stem-cells-is-grafted-onto-a-failing-heart/) asks for a regulator's approval for heart failure, which Japan has given, but conditionally, and for a different product from the one that page describes. The test for the Parkinson's cells asks for approval for use outside a trial, and so far they have been given only inside one. If either counts, it would be the second treatment in use with patients in [the group for replacing lost cells and parts](/branches/replacing-parts/), which would then count as dealt with this year rather than in 2032, my middle estimate for it now.

## The year's other results

The year's one move on this site to in use with patients was [plasma exchange](/leaves/replacing-a-persons-plasma-slows-an-age-related-disease/), which replaces the liquid part of a person's blood. On April 27th, 2026, the [Ace Alzheimer Center Barcelona](https://www.acebarcelona.org/en/plasma-exchange-alzheimers-asfa-2026) said that 71 people with Alzheimer's disease had completed its fifteen-month programme "under routine clinical practice conditions", and on October 17th I counted that as ordinary care. [The post on plasma exchange](/news/2026-10-18-plasma-exchange-in-clinical-use/) tells that story, with the case against.

The genes that can wind a cell's age back, until now tried only in animals, went into a person. On June 9th, 2026, [Life Biosciences](https://www.lifebiosciences.com/) gave [the first dose](https://www.lifebiosciences.com/life-biosciences-announces-first-patient-dosed-in-phase-1-trial-of-er-100-for-optic-neuropathies/) of its treatment ER-100, carried into the nerve cells at the back of the eye, in an early safety trial in people with two diseases of the optic nerve. On October 8th it reported the first three people with glaucoma: no serious side effects by day 56, and what it calls preliminary signs of better side vision in two of the three ([the company's report](https://www.lifebiosciences.com/life-biosciences-announces-first-in-human-data-from-ongoing-phase-1-trial-evaluating-er-100-in-optic-neuropathies/)). Three people, in a trial built to test safety and with no comparison group, cannot show that the treatment works; so far it shows only that it can be given to three people without serious harm ([the treatment's page](/leaves/a-person-receives-cell-resetting-genes-in-a-trial/)).

A planned analysis of a controlled trial reported that an ordinary multivitamin slowed a clock of biological age, a reading of the chemical marks on DNA that tracks ageing. In the [COSMOS trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02422745), 958 older adults took a daily multivitamin, a cocoa extract, both or dummy pills for two years. The multivitamin slowed two of five clocks, by about a tenth and a fifth of a year for each year of the trial; the cocoa extract slowed none ([the paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04239-3)). The authors call the effects small. They also made no allowance for testing five clocks, and say some of their findings are likely to be false, so I read it as the first result that meets this site's test for a treatment slowing a clock, not as proof that a vitamin slows ageing ([the clock entry's page](/leaves/a-treatment-slows-a-biological-age-clock-in-a-controlled-trial/)).

A drug pair meant to clear worn-out cells, [dasatinib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dasatinib) with the plant compound quercetin, beat dummy pills in a small mid-stage trial. In 31 adults with scarred fatty liver, 47 per cent of those treated had less scarring on a biopsy after the course, against 7 per cent on placebo ([the paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42255-026-01643-4)). The authors call it a proof of principle. Counted the least favourable way, with the four people who dropped out treated as failures, the result is no longer clear of chance, and the authors say a larger trial must confirm it ([the treatment's page](/leaves/aged-cell-clearing-drug-beats-a-sham-in-a-controlled-trial/)).

Two hopes did not hold up. The [ZEUS trial](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2612126) of ziltivekimab, a drug that calms inflammation, found no fewer heart attacks, strokes or heart deaths in 6,376 people, and its maker [Novo Nordisk](https://www.novonordisk.com/) closed a second large trial of it in heart failure ([its report of November 4th](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/353278/000162828026069805/caq32026.htm)); [I moved my estimate](/news/2026-11-09-zeus-moves-heart-estimate-later/) for when such drugs are in wide use from 2032 to 2035. And [metformin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metformin), the diabetes pill most often named as an ageing drug, showed no benefit on the main frailty score in a two-year trial of 145 older adults once the weight it makes people lose was set aside, though it slowed two DNA clocks slightly ([the preprint, not yet checked by other scientists](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.27.26359055v1)). The large trial meant to test it against ageing, TAME, was still described on its sponsor's page in July as "Raising Funds to Launch" ([the page as archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20260704152538/https://www.afar.org/tame-trial)).

Regulators said where they stand. At a public meeting on May 27th, 2026, run by the [Reagan-Udall Foundation](https://reaganudall.org/), which Congress set up to support the United States Food and Drug Administration, the summary records that no approved route exists for a drug whose stated use is ageing, and that no marker of ageing has been validated ([the meeting summary](https://reaganudall.org/sites/default/files/2026-07/073126_Affecting%20the%20Aging%20Trajectory%20Public%20Meeting%20Summary.pdf)). That is why [ageing as a condition a drug can treat](/leaves/a-regulator-recognises-ageing-as-a-condition-a-drug-can-treat/) is still at the first stage here, an idea only.

## What the site found

[Outrunning Ageing](https://outrunningageing.com/) went live on October 16th, 2026. Its estimate for the turning point, the year medicine starts adding more than a year of healthy life for every year that passes, has been 2058 every time it was worked out, and the chance that the turning point comes before 2050 has risen from 2.7 to 4.3 per cent ([the numbers as they stand](https://outrunningageing.com/data/headline.json)). Most of that rise came on October 17th, the day [a drug to make dogs live longer](/leaves/a-drug-is-approved-to-make-dogs-live-longer/) joined the site and an outside review of my estimates led to changes in several entries, plasma exchange's move among them ([what changed, day by day](/about/#what-changed)). More estimates moved later than earlier: of the six most-likely years I changed, five moved later. [A drug meant to lengthen the protective ends of chromosomes](/leaves/a-drug-lengthens-chromosome-ends-in-a-person/) went from 2036 to 2048, because its follow-up study found no lengthening clearly beyond chance and its one mid-stage trial was stopped, and [insulin-making cells grown from stem cells](/leaves/cells-grown-from-stem-cells-restore-a-lost-organ-function/) went from 2028 to 2032, because their maker has not applied for approval.

The most useful thing I learned about my own number is how much of it is judgment. If every treatment arrived at the earliest year I think possible and spread to ordinary people in three years, the turning point would come in 2035; if every one came at the latest and took fifteen, in 2095 ([how the year is worked out](/about/#method)). The middle, 2058, is only as good as the ranges behind it. And it waits on the two slowest groups, [damaged DNA](/branches/damaged-dna/) and [the cell's settings](/branches/cell-settings/), both at 2044 in the middle estimate, so news about them matters more here than news about anything else.

## Coming up in 2027

- **Late January to June: a second test of calming inflammation after a heart attack.** Novo Nordisk's ARTEMIS trial of ziltivekimab in about 10,000 people is due to finish collecting its main results on January 29th, 2027 ([the trial register](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06118281)), and the company expects to report in the first half of 2027 ([its announcement of July 31st, 2026](https://www.novonordisk.com/news-and-media/news-and-ir-materials/news-details.html?id=916587)). A clear benefit could move [my estimate for wide use](/leaves/an-anti-inflammatory-drug-is-approved-to-prevent-heart-attacks/) back earlier; a second failure would leave it resting on colchicine alone.
- **First half: the first people whose genes are switched off by changing their marks.** [Scribe Therapeutics](https://www.scribetx.com/) expects first results from its trial of STX-1150, a one-dose treatment that silences a cholesterol gene without cutting the DNA ([its report of November 12th](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853921/000119312526458486/sctx-ex99_1.htm)). It bears on [the cell's settings group](/branches/cell-settings/), one of the two slowest. A company's own results would not be enough on their own: [the entry's test](/leaves/a-cells-settings-are-rewritten-at-one-gene-in-a-person/) asks for a report checked by other scientists, or results posted on the trial register, showing the change lasting six months.
- **May, by the register's estimate: the main results of the ER-100 eye trial.** The trial of cell-resetting genes plans 18 people and is due to finish collecting its main safety results in May 2027 ([the trial register](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07290244)). Firmer evidence that the genes improve sight would bear on [the entry for resetting cells to reverse ageing](/leaves/partially-resetting-cells-reverses-ageing-in-an-animal/), which waits for a measured result in a person.
- **During the year: the first lifespan drug for dogs may be filed.** [Loyal](https://loyal.com/) said in January 2026 that it expected to finish the last major section of its application and apply for conditional approval of its senior-dog drug "next year" ([its release](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/loyal-receives-fda-acceptance-safety-140000887.html)). That date has slipped before: in February 2025 the company expected both of the sections then left to be finished by the end of 2025 ([the claims log](/claims/)). An approval would put "extends lifespan" on a drug label for the first time, and my estimate for it is 2028 ([the dog drug's page](/leaves/a-drug-is-approved-to-make-dogs-live-longer/)).
- **January: a trial against ageing itself is due to start.** A trial led by [UT Health San Antonio](https://www.uthscsa.edu/), with the United States Department of Health and Human Services as a collaborator, of two approved drugs, dapagliflozin and semaglutide taken as a tablet, in 1,200 adults names "Aging" as its condition; its main result is expected in December 2030 ([the trial register](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07923630)). The start date on a register is the sponsor's estimate, so whether it starts on time is the first thing to watch.
