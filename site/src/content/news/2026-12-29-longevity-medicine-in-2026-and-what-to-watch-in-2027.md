On March 6th, 2026, Japan approved two treatments made from reprogrammed stem cells: dopamine-making nerve cells for [Parkinson's disease](https://www.sumitomo-pharma.com/news/20260306.html), and sheets of heart muscle for [hearts failing after a heart attack](https://cuorips.co.jp/news-en/20260306). Each maker says no treatment like its own had been approved anywhere before. If medicine is ever to outrun ageing, it will need to replace parts of the body that wear out. By both makers' account, 2026 was the year a regulator first let a treatment made from reprogrammed stem cells be sold.

## Replacement parts, approved in Japan

Both treatments start from [induced pluripotent stem cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Induced_pluripotent_stem_cell), adult cells from a donor that are turned back into cells able to become almost any tissue, and then grown into the kind of cell a patient has lost. [Sumitomo Pharma](https://www.sumitomo-pharma.com/)'s cells are for people with Parkinson's disease whose movement problems the usual drugs no longer control well enough ([the company's announcement](https://www.sumitomo-pharma.com/news/20260306.html)). [Cuorips](https://www.cuorips.co.jp/)'s sheets are for people with severe heart failure who have run out of other treatments ([the company's announcement](https://cuorips.co.jp/news-en/20260306)). Both approvals are conditional and time-limited: the treatments can be sold, but the makers must go on showing that they work to keep them on the market.

The two have since taken different paths. Japan's public health insurance began covering the heart sheets on September 1st, 2026, and the first operation under that cover was done at a hospital in Osaka on September 29th ([Cuorips's notice](https://www.cuorips.co.jp/news-en/200261001)). The Parkinson's cells were first given after approval in October 2026, at [Kyoto University Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_University_Hospital), but inside the study that the approval requires; Sumitomo Pharma plans about ten more patients in its next financial year and a wider rollout only after that study ends ([its results presentation of October 30th](https://www.sumitomo-pharma.com/news/assets/pdf/eir20261030.pdf)).

This site follows [45 treatments and tools](/branches/), and moves each one through five stages: an idea only, shown in animals, tried in people, in use with patients, and in wide use. I have not yet decided whether either Japanese treatment counts as [in use with patients](/leaves/cells-grown-from-stem-cells-restore-a-lost-organ-function/). Each treatment has its own written test for each stage. The test for [grafts of heart muscle grown from stem cells](/leaves/heart-muscle-grown-from-stem-cells-is-grafted-onto-a-failing-heart/) asks for a regulator's approval for heart failure. Japan has given one, but it is conditional, and it is for a different product from the patches that the treatment's page describes. The test for the Parkinson's cells asks for approval for use outside a trial, and so far they have been given only inside one. The site sorts its treatments into eight groups by the kind of damage they deal with, and counts a group as dealt with in the year its second treatment reaches patients. If either Japanese treatment counts, [the group for replacing lost cells and parts](/branches/replacing-parts/) would have its second, so it would count as dealt with in 2026. My estimate for that group is now 2032.

## The year's other results

The year's one move on this site to in use with patients was [plasma exchange](/leaves/replacing-a-persons-plasma-slows-an-age-related-disease/), which replaces the liquid part of a person's blood. On April 27th, 2026, the [Ace Alzheimer Center Barcelona](https://www.acebarcelona.org/en/plasma-exchange-alzheimers-asfa-2026) said that 71 people with Alzheimer's disease had completed its fifteen-month programme "under routine clinical practice conditions", and on October 17th I counted that as ordinary care. [The post on plasma exchange](/news/2026-10-18-plasma-exchange-in-clinical-use/) tells that story, with the case against counting it.

The genes that can wind a cell's age back, until now tried only in animals, went into a person. On June 9th, 2026, [Life Biosciences](https://www.lifebiosciences.com/) gave [the first dose](https://www.lifebiosciences.com/life-biosciences-announces-first-patient-dosed-in-phase-1-trial-of-er-100-for-optic-neuropathies/) of its treatment ER-100, carried into the nerve cells at the back of the eye, in an early safety trial in people with two diseases of the optic nerve. On October 8th it reported the first three people with glaucoma: no serious side effects by day 56, and what it calls preliminary signs of better side vision in two of the three ([the company's report](https://www.lifebiosciences.com/life-biosciences-announces-first-in-human-data-from-ongoing-phase-1-trial-evaluating-er-100-in-optic-neuropathies/)). Three people, in a trial built to test safety and with no comparison group, cannot show that the treatment works; so far the result shows only that the treatment can be given to three people without serious harm ([the treatment's page](/leaves/a-person-receives-cell-resetting-genes-in-a-trial/)).

A planned analysis of a controlled trial reported that an ordinary multivitamin slowed a clock of biological age, a reading of the chemical marks on DNA that tracks ageing. In the [COSMOS trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02422745), 958 older adults took a daily multivitamin, a cocoa extract, both or dummy pills for two years. The multivitamin slowed two of five clocks, by about a tenth and a fifth of a year for each year of the trial; the cocoa extract slowed none ([the paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-026-04239-3)). The authors call the effects small. They also made no allowance for testing five clocks, and say some of their findings are likely to be false, so I read the result as the first that meets this site's test for a treatment slowing a clock, not as proof that a vitamin slows ageing ([the clock entry's page](/leaves/a-treatment-slows-a-biological-age-clock-in-a-controlled-trial/)).

A drug pair meant to clear worn-out cells, [dasatinib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dasatinib) with the plant compound quercetin, beat dummy pills in a small mid-stage trial. In 31 adults with scarred fatty liver, 47 per cent of those treated had less scarring on a biopsy after the course, against 7 per cent on placebo ([the paper](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42255-026-01643-4)). The authors call the trial a proof of principle. Counted the least favourable way, with the four people who dropped out treated as failures, the result is no longer clear of chance, and the authors say a larger trial must confirm it ([the treatment's page](/leaves/aged-cell-clearing-drug-beats-a-sham-in-a-controlled-trial/)).

Two hopes did not hold up. The [ZEUS trial](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2612126) of ziltivekimab, a drug that calms inflammation, found no fewer heart attacks, strokes or heart deaths in 6,376 people, and its maker [Novo Nordisk](https://www.novonordisk.com/) closed a second large trial of it in heart failure ([its report of November 4th](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/353278/000162828026069805/caq32026.htm)); [I moved my estimate](/news/2026-11-09-zeus-moves-heart-estimate-later/) for when such drugs are in wide use from 2032 to 2035. And [metformin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metformin), the diabetes pill most often named as an ageing drug, showed no benefit on the main frailty score in a two-year trial of 145 older adults once the weight it makes people lose was set aside, though it slowed two DNA clocks slightly ([the preprint, not yet checked by other scientists](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.07.27.26359055v1)). The large trial meant to test it against ageing, TAME, was still described on its sponsor's page in July as "Raising Funds to Launch" ([the page as archived](https://web.archive.org/web/20260704152538/https://www.afar.org/tame-trial)).

Regulators said where they stand. The [Reagan-Udall Foundation](https://reaganudall.org/), which Congress set up to support the United States Food and Drug Administration, ran a public meeting on May 27th, 2026, and its summary of the meeting records that no approved route exists for a drug whose stated use is ageing, and that no marker of ageing has been validated ([the meeting summary](https://reaganudall.org/sites/default/files/2026-07/073126_Affecting%20the%20Aging%20Trajectory%20Public%20Meeting%20Summary.pdf)). That is why this site's entry for [a regulator recognising ageing as a condition a drug can treat](/leaves/a-regulator-recognises-ageing-as-a-condition-a-drug-can-treat/) is still at the first stage, an idea only.

## What the site found

[Outrunning Ageing](https://outrunningageing.com/) went live on October 16th, 2026. The site's main estimate is for the turning point: the year medicine starts adding more than a year of healthy life for every year that passes. That estimate has been 2058 every time I have worked it out, and the chance that the turning point comes before 2050 has risen from 2.7 to 4.3 per cent ([the numbers as they stand](https://outrunningageing.com/data/headline.json)). Most of that rise came on October 17th, the day [a drug to make dogs live longer](/leaves/a-drug-is-approved-to-make-dogs-live-longer/) joined the site and an outside review of my estimates led to changes in several entries, plasma exchange's move among them ([what changed, day by day](/about/#what-changed)). Each treatment carries a range of years for when it might reach its next stage, with a most likely year inside the range. Of the six most likely years I changed, five moved later. [A drug meant to lengthen the protective ends of chromosomes](/leaves/a-drug-lengthens-chromosome-ends-in-a-person/) went from 2036 to 2048, because its follow-up study found no lengthening clearly beyond chance and its one mid-stage trial was stopped, and [insulin-making cells grown from stem cells](/leaves/cells-grown-from-stem-cells-restore-a-lost-organ-function/) went from 2028 to 2032, because their maker has not applied for approval.

I also learned how much of the estimate of 2058 rests on my own judgment. If every treatment arrived at the earliest year I think possible and spread to ordinary people in three years, the turning point would come in 2035; if every one came at the latest and took fifteen, in 2095 ([how the year is worked out](/about/#method)). 2058 is the middle of 200,000 results, each worked out from a year picked from every treatment's range, so it is only as good as the ranges. The turning point cannot come before every group is dealt with, and the two groups I expect to be dealt with last, [damaged DNA](/branches/damaged-dna/) and [the cell's settings](/branches/cell-settings/) (the chemical marks that switch genes on and off), have 2044 as their middle result. So 2058 cannot come much earlier unless those two groups do.

## Coming up in 2027

- **Late January to June: a second test of calming inflammation after a heart attack.** Novo Nordisk's ARTEMIS trial of ziltivekimab in about 10,000 people is due to finish collecting its main results on January 29th, 2027 ([the trial register](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06118281)), and the company expects to report in the first half of 2027 ([its announcement of July 31st, 2026](https://www.novonordisk.com/news-and-media/news-and-ir-materials/news-details.html?id=916587)). A clear benefit could move [my estimate for when drugs that calm inflammation are in wide use to prevent heart attacks](/leaves/an-anti-inflammatory-drug-is-approved-to-prevent-heart-attacks/) back earlier. A second failure would leave colchicine, an old gout pill already approved for that purpose, as the only such drug.
- **First half: the first people to have a gene switched off by changing the chemical marks on it.** [Scribe Therapeutics](https://www.scribetx.com/) expects first results from its trial of STX-1150, a one-dose treatment that silences a cholesterol gene without cutting the DNA ([its report of November 12th](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853921/000119312526458486/sctx-ex99_1.htm)). It bears on [the cell's settings group](/branches/cell-settings/), one of the two groups I expect to be dealt with last. A company's own results would not be enough on their own: [the entry's test](/leaves/a-cells-settings-are-rewritten-at-one-gene-in-a-person/) asks for a report checked by other scientists, or results posted on the trial register, showing the change lasting six months.
- **May, by the register's estimate: the main results of the ER-100 eye trial.** The trial of cell-resetting genes plans 18 people and is due to finish collecting its main safety results in May 2027 ([the trial register](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07290244)). Firmer evidence that the genes improve sight would bear on [the entry for resetting cells to reverse ageing](/leaves/partially-resetting-cells-reverses-ageing-in-an-animal/), which waits for a measured result in a person.
- **During the year: the first lifespan drug for dogs may be filed.** [Loyal](https://loyal.com/) said in January 2026 that it expected to finish the last major section of its application and apply for conditional approval of its senior-dog drug "next year" ([its release](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/loyal-receives-fda-acceptance-safety-140000887.html)). That date has slipped before: in February 2025 the company expected both of the sections then left to be finished by the end of 2025 ([the claims log](/claims/)). An approval would put "extends lifespan" on a drug label for the first time, and my estimate for it is 2028 ([the dog drug's page](/leaves/a-drug-is-approved-to-make-dogs-live-longer/)).
- **January: a trial against ageing itself is due to start.** A trial led by [UT Health San Antonio](https://www.uthscsa.edu/), with the United States Department of Health and Human Services as a collaborator, of two approved drugs, dapagliflozin and semaglutide taken as a tablet, in 1,200 adults names "Aging" as its condition; its main result is expected in December 2030 ([the trial register](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07923630)). The start date on a register is the sponsor's estimate, so whether it starts on time is the first thing to watch.
