Outrunning Ageing asks one question: when do we start outrunning ageing? Every year you live, medicine gets a little better, but for a person in middle age it does not yet get better fast enough, so the years you can expect to have left still fall by about a year every year. The site asks when, if ever, that stops being true, so that someone who keeps up with the best available care finds their remaining years holding steady or rising.

The question usually gets filed under science fiction or under marketing. I wanted it broken into pieces anyone can argue about, with a year at the top that is computed from them and moves in public when a new result moves it. How and why I built it is in [the essay that introduced the project](https://sentient-horizons.com/essays/outrunning-ageing/).

This is the start of the site's news section. When a reading or a ruling moves a treatment up a rung, changes the year or settles a promise in the claims log, I will write it up here beside the record it explains. A quiet week gets no post.

## Where the numbers stand

As of 18 October 2026, the middle estimate of the year medicine starts adding more than a year of healthy life for every year that passes is 2058, from [two hundred thousand runs of the tree](/data/headline.json). Nine runs in ten land between 2050 and 2069. The chance it comes before 2050 is 4.3 percent, and in no run does it fail to arrive this century.

The year is worked out from 45 treatments and measuring tools on [eight branches](/branches/), seven for kinds of damage that repair has to deal with and one for measuring ageing. A branch counts as handled in the year its second treatment reaches patients, and the year at the top cannot come until every branch is handled, plus some years for the treatments to reach ordinary people. The middle estimate for each branch:

- broken proteins and waste: handled by 2023 ([the tree](/branches/));
- inflammation and the body's messaging: 2026;
- energy and fuel sensing: 2028;
- replacing lost cells and parts: 2032;
- measuring ageing: 2040;
- worn-out cells: 2043;
- damaged DNA: 2044;
- the cell's settings: 2044.

[Six of the 45](/branches/) are already in use with patients. The gap between the clinic and ordinary people is drawn from 3 to 15 years, most likely 8.

The year has stood at 2058 at every run since the first, while the chance before 2050 has risen from 2.7 to 4.3 percent ([the headline file's history](/data/headline.json)).

## How to follow it

Every Sunday morning, Pacific time, a fresh session searches the week's news against every treatment's test, every claim and every public self-experiment, and gives each a verdict. It may move a treatment up one rung, onto "shown in animals" or "first human" only, and only on a record of the kind that treatment names. Putting a treatment in the clinic, changing an estimate or settling a claim is left to me.

Each week's reading is on [the Reading page](https://outrunningageing.com/reading/). [The Story](https://outrunningageing.com/story/) logs every paper read, and its section [What changed, day by day](https://outrunningageing.com/story/#what-changed) gives one line for each day the records changed. [The Branches](https://outrunningageing.com/branches/) lead to every treatment with its test, stage and range of years, and [the Claims](https://outrunningageing.com/claims/) page sets who promised what beside what has been shown. [The Method](https://outrunningageing.com/method/) explains how the year is worked out and who does what. If you think a range or a test is wrong, or a treatment is missing, [the Disagree page](https://outrunningageing.com/disagree/) has a form for each, and I rule on every one. [The feed](/news/feed.xml) carries every post in this section.

## The most interesting news so far

The biggest move came on 17 October 2026, when [plasma exchange reached the clinic](/news/2026-10-18-plasma-exchange-in-clinical-use/). The treatment, replacing the liquid part of a person's blood to slow an age-related disease, had sat at first human since a trial in Alzheimer's disease reported in July 2020. The first weekly reading found only the supplier's word for it, and the outside review the same day found the clinic's own: on 27 April 2026, the Ace Alzheimer Center in Barcelona said 71 patients had completed its 15-month programme under routine clinical practice. I ruled that a named clinic publishing its own practice meets the test. The case against is on the record too: the results were a conference presentation, not a peer-reviewed paper, and the blood-filtering specialists' own society still classes its role in Alzheimer's disease as not established. With plasma exchange and an anti-inflammatory drug already approved to prevent heart attacks, the inflammation branch went from handled by 2042 to 2026.

The year did not move, because [other rulings from the same review](/story/#logged-2026-10-17) pulled the other way. One was on a drug meant to lengthen the caps on the ends of chromosomes: its follow-up study found no significant lengthening, and its one mid-stage trial was stopped in 2025 because it could not succeed, so its range of years moved later, from between 2030 and 2070 to between 2038 and 2085. Taken together, that day's rulings raised the chance before 2050 from 3.4 to 4.1 percent. The review also offered four published figures for the gap between the clinic and ordinary people, and none checked out, so the gap stays a first guess.

Earlier that day [a drug to make dogs live longer](/leaves/a-drug-is-approved-to-make-dogs-live-longer/) joined the tree, at idea only, since the only data behind it so far are the company's own. A conditional approval would put it in the clinic, most likely in 2028, and adding it moved the energy and fuel sensing branch from 2039 to 2028.

On 18 October 2026, after a reading of seven papers from one laboratory, [a new measuring tool went on the tree](/story/#logged-2026-10-18): a count of the DNA in a cell's power plants, the mitochondria, taken from a blood sample, which a 2014 study of 16,401 people found predicts death and disease. It entered at first human, and the chance before 2050 rose from 4.1 to 4.3 percent.

## What to watch for

The cell's settings branch is one of the two slowest, so it holds the year back more than most. [Tune Therapeutics](/leaves/a-cells-settings-are-rewritten-at-one-gene-in-a-person/) reported at a conference in May 2026 that one dose of a treatment that rewrites the chemical marks on a single gene kept a liver virus's markers down for up to seventeen months. Once that appears in a peer-reviewed paper or as results posted to the trial registry, the treatment should move to first human. The [registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06671093) estimates the trial's main results in June 2028.

Vertex [reports its third-quarter results on 2 November 2026](https://investors.vrtx.com/news-releases/news-release-details/vertex-announce-third-quarter-2026-financial-results-november), and [the record](/leaves/cells-grown-from-stem-cells-restore-a-lost-organ-function/) is watching for any approval filing for zimislecel, its stem-cell-grown islet cells for type 1 diabetes.

A small pilot trial of [eye drops meant to dissolve the clouded protein of a cataract](/leaves/eye-drops-dissolve-the-clouded-protein-of-a-cataract/) is due to finish in February 2027, by the [registry's estimate](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07395986); no human trial of such drops has been published yet. The company behind the dog drug, Loyal, now expects to finish its application in 2027.

[The claims log](/claims/) has dates coming due as well, from 2029 on, among them the inventor Ray Kurzweil's claim of February 2026 that by 2032 "you'll gain back one year for each year lived." A claim never moves the year.
